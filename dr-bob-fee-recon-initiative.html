🔴 The 8–12 Manual Steps Per Loan

This is the current Recording Fee Reconciliation workflow, derived from CMG's internal process guide:

  • Navigate to Byte Stored Documents — 3 separate tabs (DOCS, PROP, POST CLSNG)
  • Identify which document version is the recorded one — multiple versions often exist in the same tab
  • Download the Final Closing Disclosure
  • Download the Change of Circumstance (if present)
  • Download the Final Settlement Statement / Disbursement Ledger
  • Download the Recorded Warranty Deed from the PROP tab
  • Download the Recorded Deed of Trust / Security Instrument from POST CLSNG
  • Open each document and manually locate the recording fee amount — which can be on the first page top-right, the last page bottom, or a separate attached page depending on the county recorder
  • Manually compare fee values line by line across all documents
  • If documents are missing from Byte — navigate to the Parties screen, find the Settlement Agent, draft and send an email request, wait for a response
  • If fees don't match — determine if a borrower refund is required and notify the Settlement Agent / Title Company

All of this happens before a single reconciliation decision is made. At scale, across CMG's post-close audit volume, this is an enormous ongoing time cost with compounding human error risk.

⚠️ Compliance Risk — Borrower Overcharge Exposure

Under RESPA regulations, if the recording fees charged to the borrower (as shown on the Recorded Warranty Deed and Recorded DOT) do not match the fees disclosed on the Closing Disclosure and Final Settlement Statement, a refund to the borrower is legally required.

This refund request must be sent to the Settlement Agent and the refund made by the Title Company.

The current manual process relies entirely on auditor attention and accuracy. Factors that create systematic risk:

  • Volume: Each loan requires the same multi-step process — error rate compounds with loan count
  • Fatigue: Stare-and-compare across 6 documents per loan is cognitively demanding — accuracy degrades over a shift
  • Fee location variability: Recording fee stamps appear in different locations depending on the county recorder — easy to miss
  • No systematic check: There is no automated verification that every loan was reconciled and every mismatch was caught

Dr. BoB makes borrower overcharge detection systematic and complete — every loan, every time, without fatigue.

📋 Audit Trail & Coverage Gaps

The audit trail problem: Today's evidence of recording fee reconciliation is reviewer initials on a checklist. When a regulatory examiner requests evidence of reconciliation for a specific loan, the answer is a piece of paper with a signature. This is difficult to defend, difficult to scale, and provides no granular detail of what was compared, what was found, or what action was taken.

The coverage problem: When the auditor who initiated a reconciliation job is on vacation for two weeks, there is no automated safety net. A critical finding — one that requires a borrower refund — can sit unattended indefinitely.

What Dr. BoB provides instead:

  • Timestamped automated scan log per loan — every document scanned, every value extracted, every comparison made
  • Findings record showing what matched and what flagged, with specific extracted values
  • Actor-based notification routing — initiating user + department coverage inbox
  • Bundle-type-scoped department inbox (fee-recon@cmgfi.com) — the entire team sees every finding regardless of who initiated the job
  • Findings resolution states: Resolved, Dismissed with Reason, Accepted Risk

When a regulatory examiner requests evidence, the answer becomes a complete automated audit log with extracted values, comparison results, and resolution actions for every loan reviewed.

⚡ Dr. BoB — End-to-End Pipeline

  • Step 1: Ops user logs into BoB and initiates a Fee Reconciliation bundle job (single loan or bulk)
  • Step 2: BoB retrieves the 6 required documents from BytePro Stored Documents in stacking order sequence
  • Step 3: BoB generates a single merged PDF bundle and delivers it to the designated internal server folder or SFTP
  • Step 4: Dr. BoB detects the new bundle (file watcher or polling) and begins processing
  • Step 5: OCR runs in stacking order sequence — Dr. BoB knows exactly what document is at every position
  • Step 6: Phase 1 validation — docs cross-referenced against each other (CD vs FSS vs Recorded instruments)
  • Step 7: Phase 2 validation — agreed fee values confirmed against BytePro DB as the system of record
  • Step 8: Findings report generated — pass/flag per rule
  • Step 9: Email notification delivered to initiating user + fee-recon@cmgfi.com with all flagged exceptions
  • Step 10: Auditor reviews exceptions only — dismisses, actions, or escalates each finding

For loans where all 4 rules pass, the auditor receives a "Clean — no exceptions" notification and never opens a single document.

🔑 The Stacking Order as Schema

Traditional OCR on an unknown document has to figure out what it's looking at before it can extract anything. This is error-prone and unreliable at scale.

Dr. BoB doesn't have that problem. Because BoB generates the bundle PDF in a known, fixed stacking order, Dr. BoB knows before it opens the file:

  • Document 1 is the Final Closing Disclosure — extract Government Recording Fees from Section H
  • Document 4 is the Final Settlement Statement — extract Government Recording Fees section
  • Document 5 is the Recorded Warranty Deed — scan for fee stamp at top-right of page 1, then last page, then attached pages
  • Document 6 is the Recorded DOT — scan for fee stamp at right corner or attached page

This is structured, deterministic extraction. The stacking order acts as a schema — defining what field to look for, in what document, in what location. Dr. BoB doesn't guess. It executes a defined extraction plan every time.

This is why the stacking order configuration in BytePro (Phase 0) is the critical gate — without a fixed, defined bundle structure, Dr. BoB cannot operate reliably.

📖 Source of Truth — CMG Recording Fee Reconciliation Guide

The 6-document stacking order and the validation rules in this proposal are derived directly from CMG's internal Recording Fee Reconciliation Guide — the same document that outlines the current manual process auditors follow today.

The guide specifies:

  • Exactly which documents are required for a recording fee reconciliation
  • Where those documents live in Byte Stored Documents (which tab)
  • Where to locate the fee amounts on each document (first page top-right, last page, or attached page)
  • The comparison logic — FSS fees must match the fee stamps on recorded instruments
  • The remediation path — mismatch requires refund via Settlement Agent / Title Company

Dr. BoB automates exactly this process, document by document, in the same sequence, with the same comparison logic — but without human eyes reading every page.

R1 CD vs. Final Settlement Statement

Extract from: Final Closing Disclosure (Doc 1) — Government Recording Fees section (Section H)

Extract from: Final Settlement Statement (Doc 4) — Government Recording Fees section

Comparison: All line item fee amounts must match between the two documents

Why it matters: The CD discloses what the borrower was told they would be charged. The FSS shows what was actually charged at closing. These must agree — any discrepancy means the borrower was given inaccurate fee disclosure.

Flag message: "Government Recording Fees on Final CD do not match Final Settlement Statement. CD shows {$X}, FSS shows {$Y}. Disclosure discrepancy — review required."

R2 FSS vs. Recorded Warranty Deed

Extract from: Final Settlement Statement (Doc 4) — Government Recording Fees section

Extract from: Recorded Warranty Deed (Doc 5) — Dr. BoB scans in this order: (1) top-right corner of first page, (2) bottom of last page, (3) separate attached page

Comparison: The recording fee amount stamped on the Recorded Warranty Deed must match the fee shown on the FSS

Why it matters: The fee stamp on the recorded instrument is what was actually charged by the county recorder. If this differs from what the FSS says was charged, the borrower was overcharged — a refund is legally required.

Flag message: "Warranty Deed recording fee ({$X}) does not match FSS recording fee ({$Y}). Borrower may have been overcharged. Refund required — notify Settlement Agent and Title Company."

R3 FSS vs. Recorded Deed of Trust / Security Instrument

Extract from: Final Settlement Statement (Doc 4) — Government Recording Fees section

Extract from: Recorded Deed of Trust / Security Instrument (Doc 6) — Dr. BoB scans: (1) right corner of document, (2) separate attached page

Comparison: The recording fee amount on the Recorded DOT must match the fee shown on the FSS

Why it matters: Same compliance requirement as Rule 2 — the DOT recording fee is a separate county charge that must match what was disclosed and charged to the borrower.

Flag message: "DOT/Security Instrument recording fee ({$X}) does not match FSS recording fee ({$Y}). Borrower may have been overcharged. Refund required — notify Settlement Agent and Title Company."

R4 Bundle Completeness Check

When it runs: Before any OCR begins — this is the first check

What it checks: All 6 required documents are present in the bundle PDF

Why it matters: A reconciliation is only valid if all required documents were reviewed. If the Recorded Warranty Deed is missing from the bundle, Dr. BoB cannot confirm whether the recording fee matches — so the result is incomplete, not a pass.

Flag messages by missing document:

  • "Final Closing Disclosure not found in bundle. Reconciliation cannot proceed."
  • "Final Settlement Statement not found in bundle. Reconciliation cannot proceed."
  • "Recorded Warranty Deed not found in bundle. Request from Settlement Agent via Parties screen in Byte."
  • "Recorded Deed of Trust not found in bundle. Request from Settlement Agent via Parties screen in Byte."

If a document is flagged as missing, the findings email includes the remediation path — directing the auditor to the Parties screen in Byte to locate the Settlement Agent contact for document retrieval.
